q1: What is pollution?
============================================================
Status: 200

Answer:
Pollution is the introduction of substances or energy (such as light or heat) into the natural environment in amounts or concentrations that can be harmful for humans, animals, and plants. It can reduce the health of ecosystems by harming or even causing death to living things. Pollution is most often composed of synthetic, or human-made substances, but natural substances like sediment, nutrients, and carbon dioxide can also become pollutants if they exceed a particular level, often as a result of human activities.

q3: How can land pollution become water pollution?
============================================================
Status: 200

Answer:
Land pollution can become water pollution through runoff, when rain or wind washes pollutants off the land and into storm drains or waterways. As the runoff travels, it picks up pollutants like trash and chemicals. This polluted runoff then enters streams, rivers, and eventually bays and the ocean.

For example, spilled chemicals that contaminate soil (land pollution) can be carried by rainwater and some of the contaminated soil into waterways (water pollution).

q5: How do pollutants move through the food chain and affect organisms?
============================================================
Status: 200

Answer:
Pollutants can accumulate, or build up, in an ecosystem and reach high, toxic concentrations. Plants and animals can absorb or ingest these toxins, which damages their health. As organisms eat each other, the toxins are passed from one organism to another up the food chain, increasing in concentration each time. These high levels of toxins can eventually cause death or serious health problems to the organisms.
